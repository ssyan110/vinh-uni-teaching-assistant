# Video editing and production workflow

Use this workflow for editing, repurposing, and producing video. Choose the short or long Chinese-conversation skill for classroom dialogue; follow the governing brand workflow as well for branded social content. Script-only and idea-only work does not require an editing skill.

For a code-built film in a Lemo-Opuscar style or a custom style, also use the project-local [lemo-opuscar skill](../../.codex/skills/lemo-opuscar/SKILL.md). It does not apply to ordinary editing of existing footage.

## 1. Confirm the brief and source

- Identify the audience, intended use, duration, aspect ratio, language, and the viewer or learner action.
- For course videos, identify the offering, textbook, full lesson key, approved source, and content approval state. Keep content approval, technical review, classroom playback, and release decisions distinct.
- Inventory supplied footage, audio, graphics, project files, fonts, voices, and usage permissions. Preserve originals and record their paths before editing.

## 2. Plan the edit

Write a compact treatment before building visuals: the core idea or learning outcome, opening, progression, ending, and why the chosen visual approach serves the audience. Select one or two references when useful; note the craft to learn (such as composition, pacing, camera grammar, color, or sound) and the specific elements to avoid copying.

Map each scene or beat to its source material, spoken line, shot or visual, in/out timing, caption, sound cue, and intended audience response. For course videos, preserve approved wording and plan enough time for learners to hear, read, and respond. A storyboard or rendered style frame is optional: use it when visual continuity is uncertain, the style is new, or the user requests one.

## 3. Choose the production method

Use the project's existing editor and render path when they fit the material. A code-directed, frame-by-frame film is an optional method for designed animation, motion graphics, visual explanations, or data stories. [Lemo-Opuscar](https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar) is a useful reference for style prompts paired with code-built demo films and for its directing, shot planning, sound, and review practices. Its toolkit is tuned for Claude Opus 5.5 and has a separate dependency and rendering pipeline; this project does not require installing it or adopting its renderer.

When borrowing the method, keep the treatment, timeline, and audio/caption cues connected; make the visual style serve the story or learning goal; and preserve editable source files where the selected editor supports them. Adapt choices to the project rather than enforcing film-specific rules such as a fixed hook, a minimum number of camera moves, or a sound effect for every action. For classroom dialogue, speech accuracy, speaker clarity, caption readability, and learning time take priority over visual complexity.

Before adding a new tool or fetching assets, inspect the relevant commands, runtime requirements, licenses, and project fit. Do not run a setup script or install a dependency as part of reference research.

## 4. Review and hand off

Review the actual export at normal speed with sound, then inspect a contact sheet or representative frames. Compare the export against the treatment and edit map. Check source coverage, speech and pronunciation, captions and timing, readability at the intended size, sound levels and intelligibility, speaker and scene continuity, crop, transitions, black or missing frames, ending, dimensions, duration, and frame rate. Repair identified issues and review the revised export again.

Hand off the export, editable project/source where available, captions, treatment or edit map, asset credits and licenses, and a concise record of checks and unresolved issues. Report technical verification, teacher review, classroom playback, and release as separate statuses.
