# Evidence review for lesson and assessment drafts

Use this focused review after reading the project's canonical contract, current course evidence and the relevant Boya skill. It improves a draft; it does not approve content, change the lesson source, set assessment answers, or advance an artifact state. Apply only the checks that match the task.

## Lesson activity check

1. Name the approved Can-Do, the exact student action, and the individual listening or speaking evidence the teacher can observe. For a group task, identify how each learner will speak; group output alone cannot establish individual oral performance.
2. Check the language needed to do the task: key words, question forms, ways to clarify, and length of response. Compare these demands with the cohort's taught content and observed ability. Mark an unsupported demand for teacher review; do not infer what was taught.
3. Keep the thinking and communicative goal when adding support. Give a picture, word bank, model, or short sentence starter only where it helps access the same task. The learner must still supply their own information, choice, reason or response. Remove support on a later attempt when the learner can act independently.
4. After eliciting an answer, decide what it shows, give one short response to the observed gap, and let the learner retry. Record `未观察` separately from a scored attempt. A written response or translation output cannot stand in for oral evidence.

## Assessment check

1. State what each item or oral task is meant to show and the decision its result will inform. Trace the chain from taught source and prompt to student response, answer or descriptor, score, and teacher decision.
2. Check coverage against the declared exam scope and lesson keys. Flag missing listening or speaking evidence, a weakly sampled target, and items whose answer can be guessed from wording rather than the audio or required language use.
3. Identify other demands that may affect the score, such as reading load in a listening item, unclear audio, partner dependence in an individual score, or unfamiliar task vocabulary. Change the access route or scoring interpretation while keeping the intended listening or speaking demand.
4. For oral scoring, describe observable differences between levels: what the learner understood, asked, clarified, connected, or repaired. Avoid labels such as “good” without a behavior. Keep `未观察`/NE distinct from zero and record support or prompts that affected the performance.
5. Check that two teachers could apply the answer key or rubric consistently, including acceptable variants and a borderline example. Treat classroom observations as local evidence; do not claim standardized-test validity or a calibrated proficiency level.

The result is a short issue list: **source or scope**, **observed risk**, **specific revision**, and **verification needed**. If the source, actual class performance, audio playback, or teacher judgement is missing, leave that check pending rather than supplying invented evidence.
