# Made To Stick Content

Use this as a final clarity pass for teaching material. The goal is not to make lessons flashy; it is to make the learning point easy to understand, remember, and apply.

## Hard Rule

- Do not use research jargon unless it is necessary for accuracy.
- If a technical term is necessary, explain it immediately in plain Vietnamese.
- Prefer classroom instructions, examples, and visible actions over academic language.

Examples:

- Instead of `scaffolding`, say `chia bài thành từng bước để học sinh làm được`.
- Instead of `retrieval practice`, say `tự nhớ lại trước khi xem đáp án`.
- Instead of `cognitive load`, say `học sinh phải giữ quá nhiều thứ trong đầu cùng lúc`.

## Sticky Content Filter

Before publishing, check:

1. Simple: What is the one learning point this slide or activity teaches?
2. Unexpected: What common mistake, gap, or assumption does it fix?
3. Concrete: Can students see the example, action, sentence, page, or task?
4. Credible: Why should teachers trust it? Use source lesson data, textbook page, example answer, or observable classroom result.
5. Emotional: Why should students care? Tie it to speaking better, finishing the task, avoiding mistakes, or gaining confidence.
6. Story: Can this be framed as a before/after, mistake/fix, or classroom situation?

## Rewrite Moves

- Replace abstract explanation with one worked example.
- Replace broad instruction with one visible student action.
- Replace long theory with `what to do`, `why it matters`, and `common mistake`.
- Replace cleverness with a sentence a teacher can say in class.
- Cut any term that makes the material sound smart but makes the learner work harder.

## Teaching-Material Gate

For slides, notes, worksheets, and prompts:

- One slide/activity = one learning job.
- Keep Vietnamese instructions plain and direct.
- Keep Chinese target content, pinyin, and Vietnamese roles clear.
- Use notes only when they remove confusion.
- End with the practical learning takeaway, not a textbook conclusion.
