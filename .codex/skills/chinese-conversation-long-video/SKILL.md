# Long Chinese conversation video

Read `../../../docs/workflow/video-editing-workflow.md`, `../video-reference-research/analysis.md`, and the project's current course workflow and sources. The Instagram references are 11–21 second short reels: the long-form structure below is a teaching adaptation, not an observed long-video reference. Identify offering, textbook, full lesson key, source and approval state. Follow any applicable content approval requirement before generating visuals; do not infer classroom or release acceptance from a render.

1. Define learner level, situation, goals and scope from the actual course. Structure the approved dialogue into connected scenes with an understandable opening, progression and resolution. Plan any listening or speaking task and its required materials, prompt and response time; do not pad dialogue or force repetition.
2. Write a compact treatment and coverage/edit map: learning outcome, opening, progression, resolution, scene, speaker, exact approved line, source audio/take, output in/out, caption, visual, sound cue, pronunciation check and any pause or response cue. Give each shot a reason and each cue enough time. Track all required lines and conditions; preserve complete speech and natural transitions between scenes. If voice or content is missing, flag it rather than fabricate evidence. A storyboard or rendered style frame is optional when visual continuity is uncertain, the look is new, or the user requests it.
3. Use a stable illustrated setting and recognizable characters, with an establishing two-shot and occasional speaker views motivated by changes in turn or context. Reuse character designs and props across scenes. Favor simple clean cuts and restrained gesture/expression changes; don't invent reference-specific effects. Vary framing only when it serves clarity, and give learners time to process the content.
4. Caption by dialogue turn in a readable, high-contrast safe area. Honor the class's Chinese character variant and its actual pinyin/translation requirements. Align captions to the audio and preserve full meaning; split long lines rather than shrink them. Keep answer reveals and student response intervals aligned with the intended teaching sequence.
5. Use existing HyperFrames for suitable editable compositions and FFmpeg/ffprobe for media operations; read the installed HyperFrames skill and verify runtime compatibility before rendering. Choose a suitable existing editor for character animation, footage and audio. Code-directed frame-by-frame visuals, including methods demonstrated by [Lemo-Opuscar](https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar), are optional; check their renderer, runtime and handoff fit before choosing them. Do not install its plugin or dependencies just to consult its directing and style references. Preserve assets, editable timeline/source and a versioned export. Verify licensing and permission for source assets and voices.
6. Watch and listen to the entire export at normal speed: follow the treatment and source map; verify pronunciation, caption text and timing, speaker identity, continuity, readable holds, response intervals, sound cues and levels, scene transitions, missing or black frames and ending. Inspect a contact sheet or key frames as a supplement, check the export on the intended screen, and test classroom speakers separately. Repair issues and recheck the revised export. Report technical checks, direct listening, teacher review and classroom acceptance as distinct statuses.

Positive routing: multi-scene class dialogue; an extended role-play video; editing a long conversation lesson. Negative routing: a single short reel (use chinese-conversation-short-video); HAHA brand-specific long video (also follow its governing brand workflow); an in-person slide deck (use the project slide workflow).
